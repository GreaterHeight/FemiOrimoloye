# Femi Orimoloye — Cinematic Executive Presence Film

## 1. Production objective and creative north star

Create a **24-second, eight-shot, photorealistic executive brand film** for the hero of Femi Orimoloye’s personal authority website. The film should feel like a restrained premium strategy-film opening: intelligent, composed, observant and grounded in human work. It must not feel like a generic HR advert, stock-footage montage, motivational reel or synthetic “AI corporate” sequence.

**Core brand line:** People Strategy. Organizational Performance. Lasting Impact.

**Narrative arc:** Observe the environment → establish the person → notice the details of work → reveal organisational context → show considered judgement → organise the complexity → return to human presence → resolve into the opening visual for a seamless loop.

**The story is conceptual, not documentary.** Only the supplied genuine portraits may establish Femi’s exact identity. Any generated office, meeting or process B-roll is illustrative atmosphere, not evidence that a particular meeting, client engagement, employer office or event occurred.

## 2. Non-negotiable visual standards

### Photographic realism
- Target a live-action commercial/editorial photograph aesthetic, not illustration, 3D render, beauty retouching or glossy stock-photo exaggeration.
- Preserve realistic skin pores, fine facial hair, subtle under-eye texture, natural highlights, authentic fabric weave, believable reflections, physically plausible shadows and consistent light direction.
- Skin must retain a natural brown tone. No cyan/blue wash over faces, no orange-and-teal grade, no waxy skin, no aggressive clarity, no over-sharpened edges and no fake lens dirt.
- Use believable lens behaviour: coherent focus plane, realistic depth of field, natural motion blur, restrained bokeh and no artificial radial blur.
- People must have stable anatomy across frames: two hands per person, five natural fingers where visible, consistent jewellery, stable suit buttons, no doubled limbs, no melting cuffs, no changing tie patterns and no identity drift.
- Any movement should be small enough to remain credible in a three-second shot. No fast gestures, exaggerated smiles, walking directly toward camera, dramatic handshakes or impossible camera movement.

### Art direction
- **Mood:** thoughtful, high-trust, quietly ambitious, strategic, human, precise.
- **Palette:** real-world neutral office tones, warm wood, soft daylight, restrained charcoal and navy clothing, natural greenery, balanced whites. The website’s royal blue is an interface colour, not a film-wide colour filter.
- **Lighting:** soft window daylight with plausible bounce fill, gentle highlight roll-off and controlled shadow detail. Maintain consistent direction through connected shots.
- **Composition:** preserve a relatively calm area in the left third for website copy. Keep eyes and the key subject within the central safe zone so responsive crops remain usable.
- **Environment:** contemporary but credible professional spaces. Avoid extravagant boardrooms, fake awards, visible company names, branded stationery, recognisable client logos, legible screens or implausibly luxurious interiors.
- **Typography:** do not generate words, captions, logos, signs, UI labels or legible text inside the footage. All text is overlaid by the website.

### Camera language
- 16:9 master composition, designed for a 1920×1080 delivery.
- Use restrained dolly, slider or tripod-head movement only; no drone-like movement indoors, impossible orbit shots or aggressive gimbal swoops.
- A consistent virtual camera package should feel equivalent to a full-frame cinema camera with a 35 mm lens for wide contextual scenes, 50 mm for medium portraits and 85 mm for close portrait/detail shots. These are visual targets, not a requirement to simulate camera metadata literally.
- Natural 24 fps cadence, realistic shutter blur equivalent to roughly a 180-degree shutter, no frame interpolation shimmer or unnaturally fluid motion.
- Motion is subordinate to composition. A barely perceptible move is better than an obvious effect.

## 3. Identity and reference asset rules

1. Use the supplied authentic Femi portrait as the sole identity source for any shot that shows his face.
2. If the generation system cannot preserve his face exactly from the supplied reference, do not ask it to invent a substitute face. Replace that shot with a genuine supplied photograph, a restrained still-photo camera move, or non-identifying contextual B-roll.
3. Do not infer or fabricate Femi’s age, personal mannerisms, employer premises, clients, awards or work events.
4. Generated colleagues may appear only as contextual illustrative figures. They must not resemble known named colleagues or imply a documented real meeting.
5. Do not create fake dashboards or readable data. Screens should contain abstract, soft-focus, non-legible interface shapes or remain out of focus.

## 4. Shot-by-shot generation briefs

Each shot below should be generated as a separate clip, reviewed independently, and then edited into the final sequence. Generate a few variants per shot if the tool allows; select the most physically plausible version rather than the most dramatic one.

### SHOT 01 — The room before the decision
**Timecode:** 00:00–00:03 · **Duration:** 3 seconds · **Shot type:** wide establishing frame · **Lens character:** 35 mm · **Movement:** slow lateral slider move of only a few centimetres.

**Purpose:** Establish a thoughtful, contemporary professional environment before introducing the subject. The office should feel like a real place where difficult work and decisions happen, not an architectural showcase.

**Detailed scene:** A quiet high-rise office or meeting suite in early morning. Floor-to-ceiling windows reveal a distant, softly defocused city skyline. A long meeting table sits off-centre, with a closed notebook, one pen, two plain folders and a glass of water. Chairs are aligned naturally but not unnaturally perfect. The room is empty of visible people, or any distant silhouettes must remain indistinct and non-identifiable. No company logos, signage, certificates or recognisable landmarks.

**Composition:** Keep the left third calm and slightly darker in value without applying a visible colour wash. Place the window mullions and table lines on a subtle diagonal leading toward the room’s depth. The table occupies the lower third; windows and architectural lines create quiet perspective. Avoid an overly symmetrical hotel-boardroom composition.

**Lighting and grade:** Soft daylight enters from camera-left. Highlights on the table and glass are controlled; window detail is not blown out. Warm wood and neutral grey materials remain natural. Shadows retain detail. The image should not have a blue tint.

**Camera action:** A near-imperceptible lateral slide to the right, with stable verticals and no focus hunting. The shot should feel almost still on a first viewing. Natural parallax between table edge, chairs and distant windows.

**Transition in/out:** Begin on a composition that can be matched at the end of Shot 08. End with the window light and room geometry occupying similar areas to the opening frame. Cut on a subtle movement, not a flash or whip pan.

**Avoid:** Empty luxury penthouse clichés, huge chandeliers, impossible window reflections, busy people in the background, visible text, oversaturated sunset, moving furniture, flickering windows, warped vertical lines, fake lens flares, time-lapse clouds and excessive depth-of-field blur.

### SHOT 02 — Presence without performance
**Timecode:** 00:03–00:06 · **Duration:** 3 seconds · **Shot type:** medium portrait · **Lens character:** 50 mm · **Movement:** slow push-in of roughly 2–3%.

**Purpose:** Introduce Femi as a credible, composed professional. The shot should communicate attentive judgement, not performance or staged authority.

**Detailed scene:** Use the supplied authentic portrait as the exact identity reference. Femi is seated or standing in a believable contemporary office, wearing the same approved wardrobe as the source image wherever possible. Expression calm and attentive, mouth at rest, eyes naturally focused slightly off-camera as though listening to a thoughtful question. Do not create a broad smile or a different facial expression that alters identity. Keep hands out of frame unless they are clearly natural.

**Composition:** Medium close-up from mid-chest upward, eyes near the upper third, shoulders relaxed. Position him to the right of centre so the left third remains comparatively uncluttered for web copy. The camera must not crop the crown of the head or cut through the chin. Preserve natural head proportions and the exact face from the reference.

**Lighting and grade:** Soft window key from camera-left; gentle natural fill from the room. Preserve realistic skin tone and texture, with no artificial skin smoothing. Suit fabric should show fine weave and natural folds. Catchlights should be subtle and consistent with the window.

**Camera action:** Very slow forward movement, not a digital zoom. Femi remains mostly still; a small natural blink or subtle breath is enough. No exaggerated head turn, talking mouth or hand gesture. Background shifts slightly through parallax but does not morph.

**Transition in/out:** Match the window light established in Shot 01. Cut to Shot 03 on a quiet visual relationship between the subject’s stillness and the detail of work on the desk.

**Avoid:** Identity drift, face morphing, changing hairline, changing facial hair, artificial smile, waxy skin, over-whitened teeth, extra fingers, inconsistent tie or lapel, pulsing background, robotic blinking, talking without audio, excessive bokeh and blue colour cast.

### SHOT 03 — The detail behind the decision
**Timecode:** 00:06–00:09 · **Duration:** 3 seconds · **Shot type:** close detail insert · **Lens character:** 85 mm macro-like close focus · **Movement:** gentle short slider movement.

**Purpose:** Suggest careful thought, preparation and disciplined attention without pretending to show a real confidential document or specific client work.

**Detailed scene:** Close view of a natural hand resting beside an open, unbranded notebook and a simple pen on a wood or matte neutral desk. The visible page contains only faint lines, simple non-legible marks or blank space. A plain folder and the edge of a laptop may appear at the frame edge, but the screen must not contain readable text. The hand anatomy and finger positions must remain stable throughout the clip.

**Composition:** The pen and hand sit in the lower-right or middle-right area. Leave a soft, low-detail region to the left. Focus plane falls across the pen tip and notebook edge, with realistic falloff to the background. Do not make the shot so macro that the hand becomes abstract or unsettling.

**Lighting and grade:** Natural side light with a soft highlight along the pen and paper texture. No hard spotlight, no glossy reflections on every surface. The paper is off-white rather than pure white; desk colour remains natural.

**Camera action:** Slide laterally a few centimetres as the hand makes one small, plausible movement—such as gently setting the pen down or turning a page corner. No writing of legible words. Avoid repetitive tapping or a dramatic gesture.

**Transition in/out:** Cut on the hand settling or on a line in the notebook that visually leads into the architecture of the meeting room in Shot 04.

**Avoid:** Six-finger hands, fused fingertips, impossible pen grip, moving text, legible invented words, glowing screens, floating papers, sudden page changes, impossible shadows, exaggerated slow motion and staged “signing a contract” symbolism.

### SHOT 04 — People inside the operating context
**Timecode:** 00:09–00:12 · **Duration:** 3 seconds · **Shot type:** wide meeting-room composition · **Lens character:** 35 mm · **Movement:** slow, stable pan or slider drift.

**Purpose:** Widen the story from an individual’s judgement to the organisational systems and relationships that shape work. The scene is illustrative B-roll, not a claim that Femi attended a particular meeting.

**Detailed scene:** A small group of three or four professionals in a bright contemporary meeting room. They are reviewing a few papers or looking at a display containing abstract, non-legible chart shapes. The group should appear to be in a thoughtful discussion, not posing for a stock photograph. One person listens while another gestures modestly toward a page. Use diverse professional adults with natural expressions and believable body language. No visible logos, client names, uniforms or event branding.

**Composition:** Use a wide frame that shows the group and enough room context to establish scale. Keep the people in the middle and right portions of the frame; leave the left third visually calm. Foreground objects may create depth, but should not obscure faces or become distracting.

**Lighting and grade:** Soft daylight from windows, balanced with subtle interior practicals. Skin tones are natural across all participants. The screen must remain slightly out of focus or display simple abstract blue-grey forms with no numbers or text. Keep white balance consistent with Shots 01–03.

**Camera action:** Slow slider move parallel to the table. People move minimally: a small nod, a glance between colleagues, a page moved once. Every person’s hands and gaze must remain coherent. No sudden camera arc.

**Transition in/out:** The table line or monitor edge can lead into the more personal profile in Shot 05. Use a clean editorial cut.

**Avoid:** Stock-photo smiles toward camera, staged handshakes, everyone gesturing at once, duplicated faces, repeated identical people, unreadable fake text resolving into letters, impossible laptop screens, exaggerated hand motion, logo hallucinations and artificial corporate blue overlays.

### SHOT 05 — Perspective and judgement
**Timecode:** 00:12–00:15 · **Duration:** 3 seconds · **Shot type:** three-quarter profile · **Lens character:** 50–85 mm portrait compression · **Movement:** slow micro-dolly or controlled rack focus.

**Purpose:** Return attention to Femi’s perspective: the pause before deciding, the ability to read context and consider consequences.

**Detailed scene:** Use a genuine supplied photograph of Femi or a carefully animated still-image treatment that preserves his identity. He is in a window-lit office, seen in a three-quarter profile or medium side view, looking out toward a distant city or toward a neutral off-camera point. The scene must feel contemplative, not melancholic or theatrical. Do not invent a specific employer office or a client context.

**Composition:** Place Femi on the right half of the frame with natural negative space to the left. Use foreground glass or a subtle architectural edge only if it remains physically coherent. The face should remain clearly recognisable; do not let reflections cross or distort it.

**Lighting and grade:** Window light creates soft facial modelling and a believable rim on the shoulder. Retain detail in both the bright window and the dark suit. Natural skin, no artificial rim-light halo, no cinematic teal shadows.

**Camera action:** A subtle move of only a few centimetres or a gentle focus shift from a nearby architectural edge to Femi’s profile. The face must not change shape as the focus moves. If the source image is animated, movement should be limited to camera parallax, not fabricated facial motion.

**Transition in/out:** Use a visual echo from Shot 02—the same light direction and wardrobe—to reinforce identity continuity. Transition to the desk arrangement in Shot 06 through a natural cut.

**Avoid:** New facial identity, profile distortion, eyes changing direction between frames, hair or beard flicker, dramatic window staring, visible fake skyline logos, overly moody darkness, reflections through the face and artificial lens flare.

### SHOT 06 — Making complexity legible
**Timecode:** 00:15–00:18 · **Duration:** 3 seconds · **Shot type:** overhead or high-angle desk detail · **Lens character:** 50 mm · **Movement:** slow controlled overhead drift.

**Purpose:** Visualise system design and organisation without showing a fabricated model, proprietary framework or fake business dashboard.

**Detailed scene:** A clean desk with several blank or minimally marked paper cards, a notebook, a pen and a closed folder. Two hands arrange three cards into a simple, coherent line or grouping. No readable labels, invented diagrams, logos, coloured sticky-note chaos or claims about a proprietary method. This is a metaphor for structuring a complex problem, not documentary evidence of a particular workshop.

**Composition:** Overhead or high three-quarter angle, with objects aligned naturally but not perfectly grid-like. Maintain generous negative space. Use a maximum of four or five main objects. The hands enter from the frame edge and move slowly; all shadows and object positions must remain stable.

**Lighting and grade:** Broad soft light, realistic contact shadows under paper and hands, moderate contrast. Paper is matte and slightly warm. Navy and royal-blue accents may appear only as ordinary stationery, not as a strong graphic treatment.

**Camera action:** The hands reposition one card and then settle. Camera drift is slow and straight. Objects must not slide on their own; fingers must maintain contact with anything being moved.

**Transition in/out:** End with the cards forming a calm, stable composition. Cut to Shot 07 as a return from the system to the person who must make decisions within it.

**Avoid:** Extra fingers, hands intersecting paper, cards morphing, paper levitating, moving labels, perfect floating grid animations, impossible overhead perspective, overly stylised top-down product photography and rapid time-lapse movement.

### SHOT 07 — Human presence, again
**Timecode:** 00:18–00:21 · **Duration:** 3 seconds · **Shot type:** medium portrait / editorial return · **Lens character:** 50 mm · **Movement:** restrained push-in or slow lateral drift.

**Purpose:** Resolve the sequence around a real person, not a dashboard or abstract symbol. The emotional endpoint is calm capability and approachability.

**Detailed scene:** Return to the same authentic portrait identity and wardrobe as Shot 02. Femi is in a relaxed but upright posture in a natural office environment, looking slightly toward an off-camera interlocutor. Expression is composed and open, without an exaggerated smile. If a live generated video cannot maintain the supplied identity, use the real portrait with a restrained parallax move instead.

**Composition:** Medium framing, face on the right half, enough shoulder and background to give the image space. Preserve the left text-safe region. Maintain headroom and protect the face from the edge of a mobile crop.

**Lighting and grade:** Same daylight direction, exposure and skin-tone rendering as Shot 02. The continuity between shots should be visible; do not change the wardrobe, age, skin tone, face shape or office lighting.

**Camera action:** Nearly still. A subtle breath or a small natural shift in gaze is sufficient. Avoid simulated speaking, lip movement, large gestures or a head turn that exposes an unstable profile.

**Transition in/out:** Match the posture and light of Shot 02 so that the loop feels intentional. The end frame should be able to cut or dissolve very briefly toward Shot 08 without a visible change in colour temperature.

**Avoid:** Different face, facial morph, false smile, exaggerated nod, talking mouth, unnatural eye movement, flickering suit fabric, changing accessories and overdone beauty retouching.

### SHOT 08 — Resolution and seamless return
**Timecode:** 00:21–00:24 · **Duration:** 3 seconds · **Shot type:** return to the establishing environment · **Lens character:** 35 mm · **Movement:** slow easing move that matches Shot 01.

**Purpose:** Finish the visual argument by returning from the individual to the larger environment. The closing frame should support a loop and leave the viewer with a calm, stable image behind the website headline.

**Detailed scene:** Return to the same office architecture, table edge, window geometry and light direction seen in Shot 01. The space remains quiet. If any human presence appears, it should be a soft, indistinct background silhouette and must not become a new story. Keep all objects in plausible, consistent positions.

**Composition:** Match Shot 01’s lens character, camera height, horizon, table edge, window mullions and light distribution. The last frame should be as close as possible to the first frame. Preserve the left copy-safe area.

**Lighting and grade:** Match the first shot’s exposure and white balance. No fade to black, no logo animation, no sudden vignette, no dramatic sunset shift. Highlights and shadows should align closely with Shot 01.

**Camera action:** The movement eases to the same position and speed as Shot 01’s opening. The final frame should not abruptly stop. If the source tool cannot make a reliable loop, use a carefully matched edit between the final and first frame rather than relying on a model-generated seamless loop.

**Transition in/out:** In post, compare the final and first frames side by side. Match luminance, composition and motion direction. Use a very short cross-dissolve only if needed; avoid visible ghosting in window frames or table edges.

**Avoid:** Different room layout, changed window light, object popping, camera jump, time-of-day shift, sudden exposure pulse, fade-to-black, text/logo overlays and visible loop seam.

## 6. Edit, grade and sound

- Edit in the exact order above, with eight shots at three seconds each for a 24-second master.
- Use clean editorial cuts or subtle motivated transitions. Do not add flashy wipes, glitch transitions, light leaks or motion graphics.
- Grade all shots together. Match skin tone, white balance, black level and highlight roll-off before adding any creative look.
- Keep the overall contrast moderate enough for the hero headline to remain legible without tinting the entire image.
- No music or sound is required in the website hero. Deliver the web file muted and without an audio track.
- The film should remain visually quiet beneath the headline. The text is a separate HTML layer, not burned into the film.
