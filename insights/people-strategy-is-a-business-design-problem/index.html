People strategy

People Strategy Is a Business Design Problem

When people decisions sit outside the operating model, organisations end up treating symptoms instead of designing for performance.

People strategyOriginal editorial draft

When people decisions sit outside the operating model, organisations end up treating symptoms instead of designing for performance.

The false separation

Many organisations still speak about people strategy as if it were a parallel activity: important, certainly, but separate from the choices that shape how the business competes, serves customers and allocates resources. That separation creates a familiar pattern. Leaders agree on a business direction, then ask HR to make the workforce fit it after the fact.

Start with the work

A more useful starting point is the work itself. What outcomes must the organisation produce? Which capabilities create those outcomes? Where does work slow down, duplicate or depend on a few people? What decisions need to be made close to the customer, and what should be governed centrally? These questions connect organisation design, workforce planning, talent and reward to the operating reality.

Make the system coherent

Recruitment cannot solve a role-design problem on its own. Training cannot compensate indefinitely for unclear decision rights. Incentives cannot create collaboration when the structure rewards local optimisation. Each people practice sends a signal; performance improves when those signals reinforce the same business priorities.

A practical leadership test

Before approving a major people initiative, ask three questions: which business outcome should change, what behaviour or capability must change to influence it, and what evidence will show whether the intervention worked? If those links cannot be explained, the initiative may be activity without a clear theory of impact.

The point

People strategy is not simply the management of employment programmes. It is the deliberate design of the conditions in which people can do valuable work, develop the capabilities the organisation needs and contribute to outcomes that matter.

Questions to take into the next discussion

  • Begin with the business outcome, not the HR activity.
  • Diagnose work, structure and decision rights before prescribing a programme.
  • Connect measures to the intended change and revisit them after implementation.
