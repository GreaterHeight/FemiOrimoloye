Workforce strategy

Workforce Planning Begins With the Work, Not the Headcount

A credible workforce plan starts with the work the organisation must deliver, the capabilities it needs and the constraints it must manage.

Femi Orimoloye·Original editorial perspective·6 min read

Workforce strategyOriginal editorial draft

The headcount trap

Workforce planning can become an annual exercise in moving numbers between spreadsheets: current employees, vacancies, approved positions and projected hires. These are useful inputs, but they do not answer the central question: what work must the organisation be able to do, and what capabilities will make that work possible?

Begin with demand and outcomes

Start with the strategy and operating plan. Identify the products, services, customer commitments and internal capabilities that must be delivered. Translate those outcomes into work: the activities, decisions, expertise and capacity required to produce them. Then examine how demand may change across time, geography, technology and operating conditions.

Separate capacity from capability

A team can have enough people on paper and still lack the skills, experience or decision authority needed to deliver. Conversely, an apparent capacity shortage may be caused by avoidable rework, unclear ownership or a process that makes skilled employees spend time on low-value tasks. Diagnose both the quantity of available capacity and the quality of capability before defaulting to recruitment.

Make the options explicit

A useful plan compares several responses: develop existing employees, redesign work, redeploy capability, recruit, use temporary expertise, automate suitable tasks or stop work that no longer supports priorities. Each option has different costs, lead times, risks and consequences for employee experience. Making those trade-offs visible helps leaders move from a hiring request to a decision.

Treat the plan as a management instrument

The plan should change as assumptions change. Review critical roles, capability gaps, workload signals, attrition exposure and delivery priorities at a cadence that matches the business. The goal is not to forecast every movement perfectly. It is to make the organisation more deliberate about where it invests in capability and how quickly it can respond.

Questions for the next planning cycle

Which outcomes are most sensitive to a shortage of capability? What work can be simplified before additional capacity is added? Which skills will take longest to build? What assumptions would cause the plan to change? These questions turn workforce planning from a headcount forecast into a practical conversation about execution.
