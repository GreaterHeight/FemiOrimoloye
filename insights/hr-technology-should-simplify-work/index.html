HR transformation

HR Technology Should Simplify the Work Around People

Technology creates value when it reduces friction, clarifies ownership and improves decisions—not when it digitises a broken process.

Femi Orimoloye·Original editorial perspective·6 min read

HR transformationOriginal editorial draft

Automation does not repair a poor process

Moving a confusing approval chain into software may make the same confusion happen faster. Before selecting a system, map the work people actually do: where requests begin, who makes decisions, what information is needed, where delays occur and how exceptions are handled. Remove unnecessary steps and clarify accountability before encoding the process.

Design for the people using it

Employees, managers, HR teams, finance and technology support often approach the same process with different needs. A useful solution reduces the effort required for each role while maintaining appropriate controls. That means clear language, accessible interfaces, sensible defaults, reliable status updates and a route for legitimate exceptions.

Connect data to a decision

A system may collect thousands of fields, but data has limited value if no one knows which decisions it should inform. Define the decisions first: workforce allocation, reward governance, talent development, service quality or risk management. Then establish data ownership, definitions, access controls and the minimum information needed to support those decisions.

Treat adoption as part of implementation

Go-live is a milestone, not proof of success. Users need to understand why the process changed, what is expected of them and where to get help. Training, manager communication, support arrangements and feedback loops should be planned alongside configuration and migration. If employees build workarounds, investigate the friction rather than assuming resistance is the only explanation.

Govern the whole experience

Technology choices affect privacy, security, records retention, accessibility, integrations and vendor dependence. Make these considerations part of the design rather than late-stage checks. Establish who owns configuration, data quality, access reviews, change requests and performance monitoring after implementation.

Measure friction removed

Success measures should reflect the original problem: fewer hand-offs, shorter cycle times, fewer errors, clearer status, improved self-service or more time for higher-value work. Select measures that can be observed and compared responsibly. The purpose is not to maximise digitisation; it is to make the work around people more coherent, dependable and humane.

HR transformation

Make the system easier to use.

Start with the work people need to complete, then align process, data, technology and adoption.
