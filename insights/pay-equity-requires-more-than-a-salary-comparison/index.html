Compensation & pay equity

Pay Equity Requires More Than a Salary Comparison

A credible pay-equity review examines job context, decision rules, reward structures and the quality of the data—not only salary figures.

Femi Orimoloye·Original editorial perspective·6 min read

Compensation & pay equityOriginal editorial draft

A comparison is a starting point

Salary comparisons can reveal differences that deserve attention, but a raw comparison rarely explains them. Roles may differ in scope, level, location, scarce skills, working conditions or the timing of a hiring decision. Those factors do not automatically justify a difference; they identify the context that a sound review must examine.

Define comparable work carefully

Before analysing pay, establish how jobs are evaluated and grouped. Role titles alone are unreliable because similar titles can contain different responsibilities, while different titles may represent comparable work. A consistent job architecture and clear evaluation criteria make comparisons more meaningful and help reduce ad hoc exceptions.

Inspect the rules behind the numbers

Pay outcomes are shaped by starting-salary practices, progression criteria, promotion decisions, allowances, performance processes and manager discretion. If the rules are unclear or applied inconsistently, a snapshot of salaries may show the pattern without revealing the mechanism that produced it. Reviewing policy and decision history helps identify where controls or guidance may be needed.

Treat data quality as a governance issue

Incomplete job information, inconsistent grades, outdated records and poorly defined demographic fields can distort analysis. Document the population, exclusions, comparison method and limitations. Protect personal information and restrict access to the people who need it. A technically sophisticated model cannot compensate for weak source data or unclear governance.

Move from findings to accountable action

A review should lead to decisions, not simply a report. Findings may call for corrections, clearer criteria, changes to job evaluation, manager guidance or ongoing monitoring. Record the rationale, owner and review date for each action. Where the evidence is inconclusive, say so and identify what additional information is required rather than presenting an uncertain result as a definitive conclusion.

The leadership test

Can the organisation explain how pay decisions are made, demonstrate that comparable cases are treated consistently and identify who is accountable for exceptions? Pay equity is not a one-time spreadsheet exercise. It is a continuing test of whether reward principles, operating practices and decision governance align.
