People analytics

The Metrics That Matter Connect People Activity to Business Outcomes

Useful people reporting explains what is changing, why it may matter and which decision the evidence should inform.

Femi Orimoloye·Original editorial perspective·6 min read

People analyticsOriginal editorial draft

Activity is not impact

Recruitment volume, training attendance, policy completion and survey participation can help teams manage delivery. But activity counts do not automatically demonstrate that the organisation is performing better. A useful measurement approach distinguishes inputs, operational outputs, changes in employee or organisational conditions, and the outcomes leaders ultimately care about.

Start with the decision

Before building a dashboard, ask what decision it should improve. Is the organisation deciding where to invest in capability, how to reduce critical vacancies, whether a reward approach is producing the intended behaviour or where employee friction is affecting service? A dashboard without a decision purpose can become a display of available data rather than a management tool.

Build a clear line of reasoning

For each priority, articulate the expected connection between an intervention and an outcome. For example, a redesigned onboarding process may be intended to improve role clarity and readiness; those changes may contribute to a better early employee experience. State the assumptions and alternative explanations. This makes the reasoning testable without claiming that one metric proves causation.

Prefer measures people can interpret

Define each measure, its population, time period, source, exclusions and limitations. Use consistent definitions across reporting periods. Pair leading indicators with outcome measures where appropriate, and avoid treating a single average as a complete description of different employee groups or operating contexts. Protect privacy, especially where datasets are small or sensitive.

Give the data an owner and a rhythm

Measures only influence decisions when someone is responsible for reviewing them and taking action. Establish a regular forum, agreed thresholds or questions for escalation, and a process for recording decisions. When results differ from expectations, examine the underlying conditions before prescribing an intervention.

A better reporting standard

A strong people report answers four questions: what changed, what might explain the change, why it matters to the organisation and what decision should follow? It is clear about uncertainty and does not claim that association proves cause. That standard makes people analytics less about producing more numbers and more about improving judgement.
